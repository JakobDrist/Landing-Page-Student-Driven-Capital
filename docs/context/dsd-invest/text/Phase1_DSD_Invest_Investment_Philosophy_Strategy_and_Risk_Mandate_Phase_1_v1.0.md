# DSD Invest — Investment Philosophy, Strategy & Risk Mandate

Phase 1 | Version 1.0

## Mandate authority

This mandate is DSD Invest’s governing statement of investment philosophy, strategy, risk architecture and investment authority. All subsequent research, investment modelling, portfolio optimization and Investment Committee decisions operate within its policies. It consolidates the established investment approach and takes precedence over inconsistent policy wording in earlier philosophy and operating-framework documents.

The qualitative policies stated here govern investment decisions. The regime-dependent numerical drawdown ranges are provisional research-policy parameters, with the qualifications set out in Section 5. Their inclusion does not establish finalized measurement or enforcement, empirical validation or guaranteed investment outcomes.

# 1  Investment philosophy and objective

DSD Invest is an active, fundamental, probabilistic and quantitatively constructed investor. Its objective is to outperform a broad global equity benchmark over the medium to long term through better estimates of business fundamentals, investment outcomes and risks. Performance is assessed in relation to market returns and the risk taken to generate excess returns; greater market exposure alone does not establish investment skill.

Research develops an economic understanding of companies, industries and relevant macroeconomic drivers. Analysts translate that understanding into evidence-based forecasts of fundamentals, valuation and possible investment outcomes, including their probabilities and uncertainty. Sector specialization supports depth of research, while the central portfolio framework compares opportunities across sectors and markets.

Research estimates opportunities; the portfolio model determines proposed allocations. DSD Invest does not prescribe a permanent value, growth or quality style. An investment is attractive when its prospective outcome is compelling relative to its price, uncertainty and contribution to the portfolio. Financial models, statistical methods and AI support analysis within this philosophy; human investment authority remains with the Investment Committee.

# 2  Benchmark

The benchmark is a broad, market-capitalization-weighted global equity index. FTSE Global All Cap is the current benchmark reference. Benchmark performance is measured on a dividend-reinvested total-return basis, unhedged and expressed in Danish kroner (DKK). Currency movements therefore form part of the DKK benchmark return.

The index is the performance reference. An investment fund that tracks the index is a separate investment vehicle with its own costs, trading characteristics and tracking differences; its realized return is not interchangeable with the benchmark index return.

Benchmark-relative evaluation remains consistent with DSD Invest’s regime-adaptive risk philosophy. The long-term outperformance objective coexists with a stronger capital-preservation priority in adverse economic conditions. Short-term benchmark deviation is considered in the context of the portfolio’s opportunities, exposures and risk objectives.

# 3  Investment universe

The investment universe comprises global publicly listed equities, with developed markets as the primary research focus. Research is organized predominantly by sector rather than constrained to a single geography. The permitted asset set also includes the instruments below, subject to their strategic purpose, risk characteristics and Investment Committee authority.

| Asset class | Mandate treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Global equities | Core investment universe; developed markets are the primary research focus. |
| Sovereign bonds | Permitted for justified investment or defensive exposure, including high-quality sovereign assets. |
| Corporate bonds | Permitted where the investment case and associated risks fit the strategy and constraints. |
| Cash and money-market instruments | Permitted for operational liquidity and dynamically determined portfolio allocation. |
| Gold | Permitted where its prospective portfolio or protective contribution justifies the exposure. |
| Commodities | Permitted where supported by the investment strategy and portfolio risk assessment. |
| Protective derivatives | Conditionally permitted for protection where expected benefit justifies cost, within risk constraints and Investment Committee authorization. |

DSD Invest initially operates without structural leverage or short-selling. Conditional permission for protective derivatives does not create a general permission for leveraged or speculative exposure. Asset-class permission establishes eligibility; actual allocation depends on the portfolio case and final investment authorization.

# 4  Investment strategy and portfolio construction

## Economic evidence and fundamental forecasts

Investment estimates are grounded in reliable company, financial, industry and market evidence. Historical performance informs the starting point for forecasts; changes in expected outcomes reflect identified economic drivers and new evidence. Forecasting and valuation respect sector and business-model economics rather than imposing identical metrics on fundamentally different companies.

Fundamental forecasts connect the business model, competitive position, operating drivers, financing and macroeconomic dependencies to prospective earnings, cash generation and value. Probabilistic scenarios express a range of outcomes and the assumptions that produce them. Point estimates are interpreted alongside forecast uncertainty, alternative outcomes and the possibility that the underlying thesis is wrong.

## Zero-based allocation

Portfolio construction is zero-based. Existing holdings have no automatic claim on capital: each position competes with other eligible opportunities and defensive assets on its current prospective contribution to the portfolio. Historical allocations and analyst preferences do not determine future weights.

The quantitative portfolio model calculates proposed allocations from expected returns, uncertainty, dependence between investments, diversification, liquidity and other relevant risks, within the mandate’s constraints. Position sizes are dynamic outputs of portfolio construction. Analysts supply research and estimates rather than prescribed portfolio weights.

Portfolio concentration follows the economic opportunity set and the portfolio’s combined risks. DSD Invest does not impose an arbitrary number of holdings or a fixed equity and cash split. Diversification is used where it improves the portfolio’s prospective characteristics; a large holding count alone does not establish that common risks have been diversified.

## Adaptation and consistency

Company forecasts and portfolio exposures respond to new company, market and macroeconomic information. Regime probabilities affect both prospective investment outcomes and the priority assigned to return generation or capital preservation. The model’s allocation objective is therefore consistent with the regime-dependent philosophy in Section 5 rather than a single invariant preference for minimum volatility or maximum risk-adjusted return.

The resulting portfolio is a quantitative proposal for Investment Committee consideration. The distinction between research estimates, model calculations and human authorization remains intact as evidence and portfolio conditions change.

# 5  Economic regimes and risk posture

DSD Invest evaluates Expansion, Recovery, Recession and Depression as economic regimes with different investment implications. Regime assessments are probabilistic and informed by economic evidence. Exposure adapts to changing conditions and regime probabilities rather than relying on certainty about a single current state.

| Regime | Investment objective and risk posture | Provisional range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expansion | Maximize estimated portfolio return within applicable risk constraints. Risk is a constraint, rather than an objective to minimize further once acceptable. | 50–60% |
| Recovery | Maximize estimated portfolio return within applicable risk constraints, with risk exposure increasing as recovery strengthens. | 50–60% |
| Recession | Minimize prospective loss risk and prioritize capital preservation through a more defensive portfolio. | 10–20% |
| Depression | Minimize prospective loss risk and prioritize capital preservation and organizational survival under the most restrictive risk posture. | Below 10% |

The ranges refer to preliminary average episode-drawdown research-policy parameters: average portfolio peak-to-trough drawdowns across relevant economic-regime episodes. They are not asset-allocation targets, guaranteed maximum realized losses or tested model capabilities. Precise measurement conventions, operational calibration and enforcement remain unfinalized; this mandate does not convert the ranges into automatic trading rules.

Risk tolerance becomes more defensive as expansion deteriorates and increases progressively as recovery strengthens. Allocation reflects transition uncertainty as well as the probability of adverse conditions. The established qualitative regime philosophy applies independently of the provisional status of the numerical ranges.

# 6  Investment horizons

| Horizon | Investment purpose |
| --- | --- |
| 3, 6 and 12 months | Tactical assessment of prospective returns, economic conditions and portfolio risk. |
| Approximately 36 months | Fundamental underwriting of the business, its economic drivers and valuation. |
| 5–10+ years | Strategic evaluation of investment outcomes, the investment approach and long-term capital development. |

These horizons serve complementary purposes. Tactical estimates remain connected to fundamental underwriting, while strategic evaluation gives context to shorter-term outcomes. A forecast horizon does not impose a fixed holding period or an automatic trading schedule.

# 7  Risk architecture

Risk management is integral to research, estimation and capital allocation. DSD Invest accepts ordinary equity-market volatility while seeking to prevent severe permanent capital impairment and preserve the organization’s ability to continue investing. Four complementary layers address the sources of risk that shape the portfolio.

## Forecast Risk

Forecast Risk concerns uncertainty and error in estimates of company fundamentals, valuation and investment outcomes. Economic assumptions, scenarios and their probabilities carry uncertainty. Historical evidence, alternative outcomes and sensitivity to key drivers inform the interpretation of expected returns. Confidence in an estimate depends on its evidential support; precision in a model’s output does not by itself establish reliability.

## Portfolio Risk

Portfolio Risk concerns concentration, correlations, common exposures, diversification and liquidity across investments. Individual positions are evaluated through their contribution to the combined portfolio, including shared economic, sector, factor and currency sensitivities. Portfolio construction considers how losses and liquidity needs may coincide. Diversification and position sizing reflect these relationships rather than treating each investment as independent.

## Model Risk

Model Risk concerns errors in assumptions, specifications, data and estimated relationships used to forecast or allocate capital. Stress tests, sensitivity analysis and alternative portfolio assessments challenge whether conclusions remain economically credible when assumptions change or relationships break down. A mathematically optimal portfolio remains conditional on its inputs and model structure. Neither a documented methodology nor an optimization result establishes empirical validation or reliable future performance.

## Systemic and Tail Risk

Systemic and Tail Risk concerns extreme market events, liquidity shocks and conditions that could threaten long-term organizational viability. Defensive exposure and conditional protective strategies are assessed through their prospective portfolio benefit, cost and limitations. Protection supports survival and continuity of investment activity; it does not seek to eliminate all market losses.

## Integrated risk judgment

The four layers operate together within the regime-dependent philosophy. Strong individual investment estimates do not eliminate portfolio or systemic risk, and defensive instruments introduce risks of their own. Return-seeking in Expansion and Recovery remains subject to risk and liquidity constraints; Recession and Depression place greater priority on prospective loss reduction. The provisional ranges in Section 5 do not establish a uniform maximum-loss promise.

# 8  Cash and defensive allocation

Cash allocation is dynamic and determined by portfolio optimization within the investment strategy and risk constraints. Cash competes with other permitted opportunities on its prospective portfolio contribution. DSD Invest has no arbitrary fixed cash allocation or permanent equity and cash split.

A minimum operational liquidity reserve is maintained to meet actual unavoidable obligations and preserve operational resilience. The reserve is based on the organization’s obligations and liquidity needs rather than a fixed percentage of the investment portfolio. This operational requirement constrains the capital available for investment; additional portfolio cash reflects the allocation assessment.

Permitted defensive instruments include cash, money-market instruments, sovereign and corporate bonds, gold and commodities where their use is justified by the investment strategy and risk constraints. Conditional protective derivatives are assessed on the same portfolio basis, with their protective benefit weighed against cost and their own risks. Defensive status is an intended portfolio role, not an assumption that an instrument is risk-free or protective in every environment.

Defensive exposure changes with economic conditions, regime probabilities, investment opportunities and portfolio vulnerabilities. Protection is directed toward preserving capital and the capacity to invest through adverse conditions, while remaining consistent with the absence of initial structural leverage and short-selling.

# 9  Investment governance

## Research and estimation

Analysts research businesses, industries, markets and economic conditions and produce evidence-based estimates of prospective outcomes and risks. Financial models, statistical tools and AI may assist this work. The analytical responsibility is to improve the quality, breadth and continuing accuracy of estimates; research does not itself authorize capital allocation.

## Quantitative allocation

The quantitative portfolio model compares eligible opportunities and calculates proposed positions and defensive allocations within this mandate. Its outputs express the portfolio implications of the estimates, assumptions and constraints supplied to it. Calculation of a proposed allocation does not confer investment authority.

## Investment Committee authority

The Investment Committee retains final authority over investments and investment policy. It considers research, model proposals, risks and material limitations in exercising that authority. Analysts, quantitative systems and AI support its judgment. No AI model autonomously authorizes investments, changes policy or acquires Investment Committee authority.

This mandate is the common policy reference for research, investment modelling, portfolio optimization and Investment Committee decisions. Subsequent analytical work and technical specifications give effect to these policies within the Investment Committee’s final authority.
